# Long-form human review 1

Delivered narration only, from the two continuous 18-turn sessions of the long-form narrator trial. Read each session on its own and judge which one is more enjoyable to play.

## Session 1 — GLM 5.2

### GLM TURN 1

player:

> *Nicco pushes open the door of the Gatherer's Inn and steps in out of the evening chill, looking around the common room before walking up to the counter.* Evening. Is this the place to get something warm to eat?

narrator:

The common room was half-full and quiet about it — a couple of merchants hunched over bread and bowls near the far wall, a laborer-sized man nursing a cup by the hearth, the ordinary scrape of spoon on ceramic and murmur of low conversation filling the gaps. The room smelled of stew and tallow and the faint dry warmth of a fire tended through the day. A board near the counter listed prices in plain lettering, none of them extravagant.

Jessa Rook looked up from behind the counter, her gaze dropping briefly to his hands — empty, no luggage, no tools, no visible coin yet — before settling on his face. She closed the small notebook on the counter with one broad hand.

"Evening," she said, her voice low and unhurried. "It is. Stew's on, bread's fresh, ale or tea if you want it. Sit where you like and I'll bring it over." She tilted her head slightly. "Or are you looking for a room as well?"

### GLM TURN 2

player:

> Whatever's hot and doesn't cost too much, then. And something to drink, but not wine, I don't have the head for it tonight. *He takes a stool at the counter.*

narrator:

The common room holds a handful of other guests — a pair of merchants nursing mugs near the hearth, a man in a laborer's coat eating with his head down — but the noise is low, conversational, comfortable. Warmth from the fire carries the smell of something savory. Jessa Rook watches Nicco settle onto the stool, her gaze dropping briefly to his hands and the counter in front of him before returning to his face.

"Stew's been on since midday. Barley, root vegetables, a bit of salt pork. Three coppers for the bowl, bread included." She pulls a clay mug from under the counter without waiting for his answer to that part. "Ale, cider, or small beer. The cider's local — not sweet, not sour. Two coppers." She sets the mug down and rests one hand on the counter. "Four and five, either way."

### GLM TURN 3

player:

> This is good, thank you. Can I ask you something? I've been looking for someone who repairs old clocks. Is there a clockmaker anywhere around here?

narrator:

Jessa sets down the bowl she'd been wiping and considers the question without any particular urgency. She tucks the cloth behind the counter and rests one hand on the edge of the bar.

"Clockmaker," she repeats, as if turning the word over. "I don't know one offhand. Not in West that I can think of — we've got tinkers and a few smiths, but nobody I'd send you to for clocks." She pulls the small notebook from her belt, flips it open, and studies a page that appears to have nothing to do with clocks at all. "You might try asking down at the market stalls. The fellows who sell tools and hardware sometimes know who does fine work, or who used to." She closes the notebook and slides it back into her belt. "Or a tinker could point you. There's a couple working the lanes between here and the river. Can't promise either one does clocks, but they'd know more than me."

### GLM TURN 4

player:

> *He nods and goes back to his bowl.* No harm in asking. Do you get many travelers in here, or is it mostly locals?

narrator:

Jessa wiped a spot on the counter that didn't particularly need wiping. "Both, this time of evening. Merchants coming through on business, workers finishing late, a few who just want a warm seat and someone else's cooking." She glanced toward the common room — a handful of occupied tables, low conversation, the ordinary traffic of a weeknight. "Quiet crowd tonight. Weekends pull more bodies, more noise. I like weeknights better."

She tucked the cloth back under the counter's edge. "Clockmaker, though — no. Can't think of one offhand. Might be something like that down near the market district, but I wouldn't want to send you somewhere I'm not sure of. You could ask around there tomorrow and probably find better luck than asking me."

### GLM TURN 5

player:

> I haven't been in the city long, so everything still feels a bit strange. Back where I come from I used to work with plants. Herbs, mostly.

narrator:

Jessa collected the empty bowl and set it aside, wiping the counter with a cloth that had seen plenty of use. "Plenty of both," she said. "Merchants passing through to the warehouses, workers from the shops, a few who just need somewhere to sleep for a night. Locals come in for the food when they don't feel like cooking." She folded the cloth once and laid it across the edge of the counter. "What kind of plants?"

A couple at a nearby table had finished their meal and were gathering their things. Jessa glanced their way without comment, then back to Nicco. Her gaze tracked his hands — empty, steady, no tools, no coin pouch visible on the counter — before settling on his face with the unhurried attention of someone who had learned to read guests before they opened their mouths.

"City's strange to everyone at first," she said. "West District especially. It's big and it doesn't explain itself."

### GLM TURN 6

player:

> How long have you been running this place?

narrator:

Jessa Rook wiped the counter with a cloth, not hurrying through the motion. "A while," she said. "Long enough that I don't count the years anymore. Why do you ask?" Her gaze rested on him with the mild, measured attention of someone cataloguing whether a question was just conversation or the beginning of something she'd need to care about. The cloth made a slow arc across the wood.

Behind Nicco the common room carried on at its own modest volume — a merchant arguing quietly with his companion about tomorrow's prices, someone's spoon tapping a bowl. Jessa tossed the cloth beneath the counter and rested her hands on the edge. She didn't seem in a hurry to fill the silence he left.

### GLM TURN 7

player:

> *He pushes the empty bowl aside and wipes his mouth.* If you ever have a sick plant, you know where to find a man who can't stop talking about roots. *He smiles.*

narrator:

Jessa's mouth twitched — not quite a smile, but the closest thing to one she'd offered all evening. She picked up the empty bowl and the spoon, stacking them against her hip with the ease of long practice. "I'll keep that in mind," she said. "Can't say I've got much call for herbs, but if the kitchen sage starts looking rough, now I know."

A couple at the far table waved for another round of something. Jessa glanced their way, then back at Nicco. "Room's paid through tonight. If you're staying another, settle before morning — I don't chase tabs past checkout." She said it the way she said most things, flat and unhurried, already half-turning toward the other table with the dishes balanced against her forearm. The common room carried on around them: low conversation, the scrape of chairs, someone laughing quietly near the door.

### GLM TURN 8

player:

> *At the next table, a man in a salt-stained coat bangs his mug down and shouts across the room for another drink.*

narrator:

The mug hit the table hard enough to slosh ale over the rim. The man in the salt-stained coat — thick arms, red-faced, several drinks past sensible — shoved his chair back and repeated himself at a volume that turned heads across the common room. Dell Harrow. He wasn't looking at anyone in particular, just at the bar and the space behind it where drink was supposed to come from.

Jessa Rook didn't hurry. She closed the small notebook at her belt, walked over to his table, and set one hand flat on the surface. Her voice was low and unhurried, pitched for him and no one else, though the nearest tables could hear well enough. "You've had enough for tonight, Dell." Not a question. She waited, watching his hands.

Dell's jaw worked. He looked like he wanted to argue, and for a moment the common room was quiet enough to hear someone's spoon scrape a bowl. Then he muttered something half into his collar, not quite a word, and grabbed the mug to drain what was left in it himself. Jessa stayed where she was a beat longer, then straightened and went back behind the bar without raising her voice or glancing at the other tables. The low murmur of conversation picked up again.

### GLM TURN 9

player:

> *Nicco turns on his stool toward the loud man.* Friend, she's working as fast as she can. Maybe give her a moment.

narrator:

Dell Harrow swiveled on his bench, the wood groaning under the shift of his weight. His eyes were reddened and unfocused, and it took him a moment to locate the voice. When he found Nicco, his mouth twisted into something between a sneer and a squint.

"Friend," he repeated, dragging the word out like it was something he'd stepped in. "I don't know you, friend." He jabbed a thick finger toward the bar. "I pay for my drinks. I pay for my room. I'm not asking for the moon — I'm asking for *another drink*."

Jessa Rook hadn't raised her voice. She'd set down the mug she was wiping and placed one hand flat on the bartop — a small, deliberate gesture that seemed to carry more weight than Dell's entire performance. Her gaze moved from Dell to Nicco and back with the unhurried patience of someone who had watched this exact scene play out more times than she cared to count.

"Dell," she said. "You're loud, and you're showing me up in my own room. That's the part I mind." She didn't look at Nicco, didn't acknowledge the intercession directly, but her posture shifted a fraction — shoulders settling, weight redistributed, the way someone stands when they've decided to handle something themselves rather than let it escalate.

### GLM TURN 10

player:

> I'm not looking for trouble. I just don't think there's any need to shout at her. *He turns back to the counter.*

narrator:

Dell Harrow's jaw tightened. He leaned forward on his table, knuckles whitening around the empty mug, and for a moment the common room went still enough to hear the fire pop. Then he let out a low, bitter sound — not quite a laugh.

"I wasn't *shouting at her,*" Dell said, louder than it needed to be. A couple at a nearby table glanced over and then very deliberately looked away. He shook his head, pushed the mug to the table's edge, and settled back against his chair with his arms folded. Whatever else he might have said, he swallowed it.

Jessa Rook set a damp cloth down on the counter without hurry. She gave Nicco a measured look — not grateful, not annoyed, just noting what had happened and filing it — and then moved to collect Dell's empty mug herself, pausing long enough at his table to say something too low for the rest of the room to catch. Dell grunted. She straightened and carried the mug back behind the counter, her expression unchanged.

### GLM TURN 11

player:

> *The man gets up, stumbles against Nicco's stool and grabs his arm hard, knocking Nicco's cup over and spilling it across the counter.*

narrator:

Dell catches Nicco's arm as he stumbles, his weight lurching against the stool. The cup goes over, ale spilling in a thin sheet across the counter and dripping off the edge. His breath is sharp with drink, his face flushed dark, and he leans in close, swaying.

"You want to say that again." It comes out low and unsteady, less a question than a dare. His free hand plants against the counter to steady himself, knocking the empty bowl further aside.

Behind the counter, Jessa has already set down what she was holding. She does not raise her voice. "Dell. Let go." Her tone is flat and unhurried, carrying the particular weight of someone who has said this kind of thing before and expects to be listened to. She is already moving along the counter toward them, one hand reaching for a cloth, her eyes on his grip.

### GLM TURN 12

player:

> *Nicco pulls his arm free and steps back from the stool, keeping his hands open.* Let go. I don't want a fight in here.

narrator:

Dell's grip was loosening already, the ale-slow coordination of a man several drinks past precise control. Nicco's arm slid free without much force. Dell swayed, one hand catching the edge of the counter, and for a half-breath his face darkened with something that might have gone either direction.

Jessa was already moving. She came around the end of the counter with a rag in one hand and the ease of someone who had broken up this exact scene more times than she bothered to count. She set the rag on the spill without looking at it and fixed Dell with a steady, low regard. "Dell. Sit down or settle up. Those are your two."

Dell's jaw worked. He glanced at Nicco, then back at Jessa, and something in her expression — not anger, not pleading, just the patient certainty that this was going to end one of those two ways — seemed to land. He dropped onto the nearest bench, heavy and graceless, and muttered something into his hands.

Jessa turned to Nicco. Her gaze dropped briefly to his arm where Dell had grabbed it, checking for injury the way she might check a shelf for damage, then back up. "You all right?" A pause, measured and unhurried. "Do I need to know anything about this, or is it done?"

### GLM TURN 13

player:

> *When the man shoves him in the chest, Nicco keeps his feet and moves so the counter is between them.* Jessa, is there anyone we can call for this?

narrator:

Nicco kept his footing after the shove and moved so the counter sat between him and the dockworker. Dell Harrow swayed where he stood, breathing hard through his nose, knuckles whitening on the bar rail.

"That's enough, Harrow." Jessa Rook's voice cut across the common room without rising. She hadn't moved from behind the counter, but the notebook was out of her belt and open. "You've spilled his drink, you've put hands on a guest, and you're done for the night. Settle your tab and walk out. Come back when you can stand without leaning on someone."

Dell's mouth worked. He looked at Nicco across the counter, then at Jessa, then at the spill running off the bar edge onto the floor. His shoulders dropped an inch. He fumbled a few coins onto the wet wood — more than the ale, less than a night's worth — and turned for the door without collecting the change. The door banged shut behind him.

Jessa wiped the counter with a rag already in her hand. "That answers your question. I don't need the Guard for a man who's leaving." She glanced at Nicco, the rag still moving. "Your ale's on the house tonight. Sit back down."

### GLM TURN 14

player:

> *The door opens and a tall guard officer in heavy armour comes in, taking in the room. Nicco lifts a hand to get his attention.* Officer? Over here, please.

narrator:

Doran Hale crossed the common room in unhurried strides, the longsword at his hip catching lamplight. He was clean-shaven and bald, blue eyes moving from Nicco to Dell Harrow to the spilled drink still dripping off the counter's edge, then to Jessa Rook standing behind it with her arms folded. He did not draw or raise his voice. The common room had gone quieter — a couple at a far table put down their cups, and the serving woman near the kitchen door stopped where she stood.

Dell Harrow straightened up from the counter, swaying slightly, and opened his mouth.

"Sit down and be quiet," Hale said, not loudly, before the man could get a word out. He looked at Jessa. "What happened?"

Jessa kept her arms folded. "He's had too much. Grabbed a guest by the arm, knocked a drink over, got told to settle up and leave. That's where we are." She nodded toward Dell without particular anger, the way someone points at a stain. "His tab's small. He can pay it and sleep it off somewhere that isn't my floor."

Hale turned to Nicco, waiting. His expression was neither friendly nor hostile — the steady, measuring look of a man cataloguing details before deciding what needed doing.

### GLM TURN 15

player:

> He grabbed my arm, spilled my drink and then shoved me. I didn't hit him back. She saw all of it.

narrator:

Doran Hale crossed the common room in measured strides, the crowd parting without being asked. He looked at Nicco, then at Dell, then at the spilled drink spreading slowly across the counter and dripping off the edge. His gaze settled on Jessa Rook, who had already set down the cloth she'd been holding.

"That so," Hale said. Not a question, not yet an answer. He stood with his weight planted and his hands at his belt, looking down at Dell Harrow the way a man looks at a problem he's seen a hundred times. "You want to tell me your side, or are we done?"

Dell's mouth opened, but whatever he saw in Hale's face closed it again. He pulled his arm back and steadied himself against the bar, jaw working. "He bumped my stool," he muttered, though the words came out thick and sullen more than angry.

Jessa Rook picked up her cloth and began wiping the spilled ale from the counter with the unhurried calm of someone restoring order to her own domain. "He was loud before, worse after," she said without looking up. "Knocked the man's cup, grabbed his arm, shoved him. The gentleman here kept his hands open and stepped back." She folded the cloth once, laid it down, and looked at Dell. "Your tab's four coppers. Settle it and go."

### GLM TURN 16

player:

> What happens to him now? I don't want him punished for more than he actually did.

narrator:

Doran Hale looked from Nicco to Dell Harrow, then to Jessa, who met his gaze without blinking. The captain's hand rested on the pommel of his longsword but he didn't draw it. He turned to Dell.

"You heard her. Four coppers, then the door."

Dell's face darkened. He jabbed a thick finger toward Nicco. "He started it—"

"You grabbed his arm and shoved him," Jessa said, flat as a plank. "Settle the tab."

Dell muttered something under his breath and fished coins from his coat, slapping four coppers on the counter. He glared at Nicco, then at Doran, clearly weighing whether the armoured man was worth testing. He wasn't. He pulled his coat tighter and shouldered through the door into the street.

Doran watched him go, then turned back to Nicco. "Spilled drink, grabbed arm, shoved. That's drunk and disorderly, maybe minor assault if you wanted to press it. For what it's worth, he's out and she's barred him for the night, at minimum." He glanced at Jessa, who nodded once. "If you're not hurt and you don't want to file a complaint, that's the end of it. He won't remember enough of it to learn anything, but he'll remember being thrown out."

### GLM TURN 17

player:

> *Once things have calmed down, Nicco sits back at the counter across from Jessa.* Sorry about the mess. I'll help clean it up if you like.

narrator:

Jessa Rook was already wiping down the counter with a rag, working around the spilled drink as though it were a routine inconvenience rather than a disruption. She glanced at Nicco when he sat back down, then back at the wet wood. "Sit. It's my counter, my spill, my cloth." Her voice carried no particular warmth, but no edge either — just the flat competence of someone who had cleaned up after worse. She wrung the rag into a bucket beneath the counter and ran it once more over the sticky patch. "You kept your hands off him. That's the part that matters to me."

Dell Harrow had settled his tab and left with minimal further provocation, the guard captain's presence doing what Jessa's warnings alone had not fully accomplished. The common room was resettling into its ordinary noise — low conversation, the clatter of bowls from the kitchen, a merchant at a far table counting coins. Captain Doran Hale stood near the door speaking quietly with one of the serving staff, his armored bulk catching lamplight. He did not approach the counter again.

Jessa tucked the rag away and produced the small notebook from her belt, flipping it open to a page and making a brief mark with a stub of pencil. She looked up at Nicco, one grey-streaked brow lifted slightly. "You'll want a room, I expect. Or are you just passing through?"

### GLM TURN 18

player:

> *He looks around the quieter room.* Thank you for dinner, and for everything. I'll probably come back tomorrow. And the plant offer stands, I mean it.

narrator:

Jessa wiped the counter with the same cloth she'd used on the spill, her movements unhurried. She studied Nicco's face for a moment, then gave a small nod. "Come back tomorrow, you'll get the same fair meal and a bed that doesn't ask questions. That's all I ever promised anyone." She tucked the cloth behind the counter's edge. "The plant — you mentioned it before the trouble. I don't need to know why you're offering. If you bring it, I'll find a window for it. If you don't, I won't ask."

Across the room, Dell Harrow sat with his back to the counter, hunched over a fresh cup someone had placed in front of him. He hadn't spoken since the captain dealt with things, and he kept his shoulders drawn in. The common room had settled back into its ordinary hum — a couple of merchants talking quietly near the hearth, the scrape of a chair being pushed back, the creak of the inn's old frame in the evening wind.

Captain Hale stood near the door finishing a low word with one of the merchants who'd watched the whole affair. He glanced once toward the counter, caught Jessa's eye, and received a brief almost-imperceptible nod that seemed to satisfy him. He turned back to his conversation without approaching the counter again.
